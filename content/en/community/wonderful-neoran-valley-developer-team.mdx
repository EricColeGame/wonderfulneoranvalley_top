The **Wonderful Neoran Valley developer team** is Nara Studio, the independent studio building the game. Its public campaign page names eleven people and lists the role each one holds, from creative direction and narrative to rigging, VFX, and technical audio.

That roster is the clearest picture currently available. Nara Studio has not published a full company directory, so the credits below should be read as the team as the studio presents it, not as an exhaustive headcount.

## Who is on the Wonderful Neoran Valley developer team?

## What the team says about how it builds the valley

Nara Studio describes its creature and environment work as an attempt to blend fantasy with real-world pop culture, folklore, and mythology. The stated goal for each Neoran is a design that explains itself at first sight, so a player can read what kind of creature they are facing without opening a codex.

The studio also explains its choice of 3D graphics. It calls 3D the most fitting tool for the story's more allegorical moments, and frames the decision as an artistic challenge: making a quality monster collector without falling back on a two-dimensional look. The team states plainly that no AI was used for any of the game's art or creative assets.

Audio sits with the same small group rather than an outside contractor, at least on paper. Cilurzo is credited as composer and Eusepi as sound designer and technical audio, and the studio says the soundtrack transitions seamlessly between exploration and combat.

## How the team's design goals show up in the game

The systems the studio has described follow from its stated worry that monster collectors can reward stagnant, turn-by-turn play. The Knight's foresight feeds a Timeline showing enemy actions several turns ahead, so battles are framed as planning problems rather than reactions.

Around that core, the confirmed systems include Synapse, which lets party members act during the planning phase using Neura charges that build over turns; Jewels, team-wide passive artifacts forged by Iris; Sigils, which modify individual Neoran Actions; and Traits, passive modifiers with some field-wide effects.

Recruitment is dialogue-based. The team has been explicit that you do not catch Neoran with a device — you convince them on the battlefield, and different species respond differently to the Knight's approach. Mehtyr, for example, is described as dismissive of anyone who cannot match its objections.

## Voice cast and campaign-funded additions

Two cast members are confirmed: Troy Baker voices the Knight, and Ben Starr voices Iris. Professional voiceover was also listed as a campaign stretch goal, and the campaign page's own text indicates all stretch goals were reached.

Other stretch goals touched the team's workload rather than its headcount. A Nintendo Switch 2 port, a PS5 port, French and Spanish localization, additional Neoran, extra questlines, and several modes were all pitched as additions funded by the campaign. The confirmed platform list now covers PC via Steam, PlayStation 5, and Nintendo Switch 2, with English, French, Spanish, and Italian at launch.

None of this is a release date. The studio is targeting 2028, and no exact day has been announced.

## Can backers actually meet the developer team?

Yes, through the campaign's Premium Co-Design tiers. Those tiers include four one-hour meetings with members of the developer team.

The structure is described in stages: the first meeting is a pitch session where the backer and the team define key design elements together. The next two meetings show progress from designers and artists, with feedback collected along the way. In the fourth meeting, the backer sees the final version of their creation before anyone outside the studio.

The studio keeps final say. Balancing decisions and any judgment on whether a co-designed item is appropriate rest with Nara Studio's designers, which the campaign states directly.

## What is not confirmed about the team

Some reasonable questions have no answer in the public material yet. The studio has not published a total headcount beyond the eleven credited names, a studio location, a hiring roadmap, or a breakdown of who handles publishing and community work.

The stretch goal for full independence describes producing the game without seeking additional publisher funding, which speaks to the studio's intentions but not to its internal structure. Treat any claim about team size, outside contractors, or unlisted staff as unconfirmed until Nara Studio says otherwise.
