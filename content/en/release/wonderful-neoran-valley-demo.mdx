## How the Roguelite Systems Work

A demo is not the only way to understand what the game is. The official site documents its combat loop in detail, and it is not a standard creature-collector formula.

Combat revolves around the **Timeline**. The Knight has the gift of foresight, which reveals enemy Neoran actions several turns in advance. You plan offensive and defensive sequences against that forecast, weighing the order and synergy of your actions rather than simply picking the strongest move.

**Synapse** is the duo's signature power. Iris binds the Neura flow in the Knight's arm to a Neoran on the field, letting party members act during the planning phase — before the Timeline starts. Synapse actions consume Neura charges that build turn after turn, so timing matters.

**Jewels** are Neura artifacts forged by Iris that passively buff the whole team. **Sigils** modify individual Neoran Actions, and **Traits** are passive modifiers, some with effects that apply to every creature on the field.

## Neorans Confirmed for the Roster

The game promises more than 70 recruitable Neoran at base scope, with campaign stretch goals adding more. Recruitment happens through dialogue on the battlefield, not by throwing a capture device, and Neoran evolve.

Only a handful of creatures are documented so far:

- **Pero** — a famously simple creature that communicates only by spinning, lives its early life on tree branches, and sits at the bottom of the valley food chain.
- **Yakid** — a durable coastal clan creature that serves its clan's Wakiza.
- **Sagira** and **Biskem** — both named in the official Synapse example, where a wild Biskem prepares a Cloudbreak with a +300% bonus and Sagira's Silent Assassin is used to neutralize it.

Beyond these, roster details remain unconfirmed.
