Wonderful Neoran Valley Neorans are the creatures at the heart of Nara Studio's narrative-driven roguelite monster collector. The official material confirms that Neoran are intelligent, many can speak the human language, and some are dangerous. It also makes clear that not every Neoran fits that description: Pero is the deliberate exception. The game is targeting 2028 for PC via Steam, PlayStation 5, and Nintendo Switch 2. Official details are available at [wonderfulneoranvalley.com](https://wonderfulneoranvalley.com/). Here is what is confirmed about the Neorans, how they join your party, and how they shape combat.

## What Are Neorans in Wonderful Neoran Valley?

In the official site's framing, Neoran are intelligent creatures. Most can speak the human language, and some can be dangerous, but those definitions do not apply to all of them. That caveat matters because it leaves room for exceptions such as Pero, whose description deliberately breaks the rule.

The game is a roguelite monster collector, so Neoran are both world-building characters and party members. Recruitment happens on the battlefield through dialogue, not by throwing a capture device. The planned base scope promises more than 70 recruitable Neoran, with campaign stretch goals intended to add more.

## Confirmed Neorans and Characters So Far

The official material names several creatures, but not all receive full profiles. Pero and Yakid have clear creature descriptions. Sagira and Biskem appear in gameplay examples. Iris is a lead character and jewel-forger, not presented as a recruitable Neoran on the official page.

### Pero

Pero is the official example of a Neoran that does not fit the intelligent, speaking norm. Its brain is described as extremely underdeveloped, and it communicates only by spinning around. Peros begin life on the branches of the tree where they are born. At maturity they fall to the ground and become easy prey, placing them at the bottom of the valley food chain.

The official description adds that Pero is fearless even against deadly predators because fear is too complex for its tiny, smooth brain.

### Yakid

Yakid is a confirmed Neoran in the official roster. It is described as a durable coastal clan creature that serves its clan's Wakiza. Beyond that role, the supplied material does not detail Yakid's stats, evolution, or signature actions, so those points remain unconfirmed.

### Sagira, Biskem, and Other Named Creatures

The official gameplay examples name Sagira and Biskem. Sagira's Silent Assassin is cited as a way to neutralize a wild Biskem's Cloudbreak, which was about to deal +300% bonus damage. Those examples confirm the names and actions, but they do not provide full Neoran profiles, recruitment requirements, or evolution paths. Treat them as named examples rather than complete roster entries.

## How Recruitment Works: Dialogue, Not Capture

Recruitment is dialogue-based and happens on the battlefield. The official material does not describe a capture-device loop or capture rates. Instead, Neoran are recruited one by one along the journey, and each brings its own arsenal of Actions.

That distinction matters for expectations: Wonderful Neoran Valley Neorans are characters you talk to and add to the party, not creatures you trap in a device. The official FAQ also confirms that Neoran evolve, though it does not yet detail evolution methods or forms.

## Evolution and Roster Scope

Evolution is confirmed, but the official FAQ gives only a one-word answer: yes. No evolution levels, branch paths, or form counts are provided.

Roster scope is clearer at the base level. More than 70 recruitable Neoran are promised at base scope, with campaign stretch goals intended to add more. That figure is a planned scope, not a final launch roster. The game is targeting 2028, and the launch languages are English, French, Spanish, and Italian, with more localizations possibly added later.

## How Neorans Shape Combat

Combat revolves around the Timeline, which the Knight can use thanks to foresight to see enemy Neoran actions several turns ahead. Party composition matters because each recruited Neoran adds Actions, and Synapse gives every Neoran its own planning-phase action with a unique effect and animation. Synapse consumes Neura charges that build turn after turn.

Jewels are team-wide passive artifacts forged by Iris. Sigils modify individual Neoran Actions. Traits are passive modifiers, some with field-wide effects. The result is a roguelite where order and synergy matter, not just which creature you choose.
