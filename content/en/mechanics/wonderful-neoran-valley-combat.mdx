Wonderful Neoran Valley combat is built around planning, not throwing a capture device. Nara Studio describes its game as a narrative-driven roguelite monster collector in which the Knight reads enemy intentions, recruits Neoran through battlefield dialogue, and builds a team whose members support one another. The game is targeting 2028 on PC via Steam, PlayStation 5, and Nintendo Switch 2. The confirmed combat picture is specific: foresight, a Timeline, resource efficiency, and synergy matter more than brute force.

## Foresight and the Timeline Shape Combat

The Knight’s defining power is foresight. In combat, this appears through the Timeline, which shows enemy Neoran actions several turns ahead. That information is meant to let you plan an offensive or defensive sequence rather than react one turn at a time.

Nara Studio frames the goal as efficiency, not merely survival. A battle should be completed while spending as few attacks, items, and HP as possible. The studio’s example contrasts wasting a strong move on a lowly Pero with needing Sagira’s strongest move later. The point is to leave resources ready for the next fight in the run.

This is confirmed combat design. The supplied material does not describe a real-time action system, and it does not confirm capture rates or type matchups.

## Recruitment Is a Battlefield Conversation

You cannot “catch” Neoran as in a typical monster collector. Recruitment happens through dialogue when you meet a Neoran on the battlefield. Each creature reacts differently to the Knight, and knowing its desires and preferences is essential to winning favor.

Mehtyr is the source’s example: it will resist joining if you cannot match its objections and may lose interest if you do not take a firm stand. Other Neoran may react badly to tough love. That makes recruitment part of combat planning rather than a separate collection minigame.

Yakid is a confirmed roster creature and illustrates the game’s creature design. It is described as physically resilient, devoted to its clan’s Wakiza, and part of coastal clan conflicts. The evidence does not provide numerical stats or a recruitment formula for Yakid.

## Synergy Rewards Careful Team Building

The Timeline is tied to team synergy. Nara Studio says the combat system heavily rewards Neoran that thrive off each other’s presence, rather than betting everything on one powerful creature. Some unique effects come only from chaining specific moves or switches.

Switching can also trigger brief narrative sequences between Neoran without interrupting battle flow. Those moments are a way to learn about relationships between species and to think about how their traits combine. The Knight is described as a master tactician, more skilled at directing others through combat than as a lone fighter.

Base scope promises more than 70 recruitable Neoran, and campaign stretch goals would add more. That breadth supports experimentation, but the supplied evidence does not list every synergy or switch effect.

## Synapse, Jewels, Sigils, and Traits

Four confirmed systems add layers to team planning. They are not interchangeable.

| System | Confirmed role |
| --- | --- |
| Synapse | Lets party members act during the planning phase and consumes Neura charges that build over turns. |
| Jewels | Team-wide passive artifacts forged by Iris. |
| Sigils | Modify individual Neoran Actions. |
| Traits | Passive modifiers; some have field-wide effects. |

These systems explain why combat is described as layered rather than a simple attack exchange. They also reinforce the game’s stated focus on planning and synergy. The supplied material does not provide a full list of Jewels, Sigils, or Traits, so specific builds should be treated as unconfirmed until Nara Studio details them.

## Resource Planning Across the Run

Combat efficiency carries into the roguelite run. Each adventure is meant to present different challenges, and knowledge from one run helps the next. The source mentions new Neoran, items, and synergies to discover.

Shops matter during a run. Syllva’s shop can heal your Neoran for a fee of Crystal Neura. Other shops sell items or enhance the team’s offensive or defensive capabilities. Neura Clusters are another run-level threat: they distort nearby areas and make some Neoran hostile, and destroying them is part of the stated loop.

Kickstarter stretch goals list combat- and run-focused modes such as Challenge Mode, Hard Mode, Endless Mode, Brawl Mode, First Try Mode, and Randomizer Mode. Treat these as campaign plans, not confirmed launch specifics. The supplied evidence does not confirm an exact release date, price, or public demo.
