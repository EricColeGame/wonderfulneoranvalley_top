**Wonderful Neoran Valley** is a roguelite monster collector in development at Nara Studio, and the phrase "monster collector" does a lot of work here. The creatures you gather are not pulled out of tall grass with a device. They are intelligent beings, many of them capable of speech, and you bring them into your party through conversation on the battlefield. That single design choice reshapes everything around it — how combat is planned, how runs are built, and why recruiting one more Neoran matters.

This guide sticks to what Nara Studio has actually shown and stated about the game's systems.

## What "monster collector" means in Wonderful Neoran Valley

The official FAQ describes the genre plainly: Wonderful Neoran Valley is a roguelite monster collector. The studio's own material also establishes that Neoran are intelligent creatures, most of whom can speak the human language, and that some of them can be dangerous. Those definitions do not apply to every creature — Pero is the standing exception — but the baseline is intelligence, not instinct.

Recruitment follows from that. Neoran join through dialogue on the battlefield rather than through a capture device. There is no documented capture rate, no weakened-state capture step, and no throwing mechanic in the material released so far. If you are coming from creature-collecting games built on capture loops, expect the recruitment conversation to be the moment of acquisition instead.

Evolution, however, is confirmed. The official FAQ answers "Do Neoran evolve?" with a direct yes, though the specific evolution paths have not been published.

## The Timeline makes every recruit a planning tool

Combat in Wonderful Neoran Valley revolves around the Timeline. The Knight — the player character — carries the gift of foresight, which lets you see enemy Neoran actions several turns in advance. The Timeline is where that foresight becomes a decision.

Because you can see what is coming, the interesting question is not only which actions to slot in, but in what order and with what synergy. Nara Studio states that these combinations differ from run to run, which is the roguelite layer sitting on top of the collector layer. Enemy Neoran often build their own combos inside their portion of the Timeline, so reading their sequence tells you where the plan can be broken.

Resources are finite within a run. The studio's framing is blunt: every resource spent today will be missing tomorrow. Beating the Neoran in front of you is only half the job; the other half is arriving at the next fight still capable of winning it.

## Synapse: the reason a new Neoran changes your whole turn

Synapse is the collaboration power shared by the Knight and Iris, the baby dragon who serves as the game's second lead. Iris binds the Neura flow in the Knight's arm to that of a Neoran on the field, extending the Knight's time-altering ability to the entire party. Party members become able to act through and against time.

In practice, Synapse actions can be performed before the Timeline starts, during the planning phase, and they alter the order, magnitude, and number of incoming enemy attacks. Every Neoran has its own Synapse action with a unique effect and animation. That is the mechanical heart of the collector loop: recruiting a creature adds a new way of bending time, not just a new stat line.

Synapse actions consume Neura charges, which build turn after turn. Spending them immediately or saving them for a larger play is a real decision each battle.

The trailer example Nara Studio cites: a wild Biskem is about to land a Cloudbreak with a +300% bonus. Rather than spend a defensive action, the Knight neutralizes it with Sagira's Silent Assassin for two Neura charges. The studio has also teased that Synapse can be performed between the Knight and Iris themselves, without revealing the effect.

## Jewels, Sigils, and Traits: three separate build layers

These are the systems that define a run, and they operate at different scopes.

**Jewels** are Neura artifacts forged by Iris. They grant passive bonuses to your whole team and, according to the studio, will be the largest single factor in defining your build each run. The Steam Jewel shown in the trailer boosts Blue attacks and Red attacks when those are placed next to each other in your Timeline — a positional condition, not a flat buff.

**Sigils** are modifiers applied to individual Neoran Actions. They increase strength or change how an action behaves. They are described as less common than Jewels but more adaptable, which makes them the tool for squeezing versatility out of a creature you already rely on.

**Traits** are passive modifiers, and some carry global effects that apply to every creature on the field — including enemies. That last detail matters. A Trait is not automatically a pure upgrade to your side of the board, and reading the enemy's setup means accounting for the same field-wide rules.

The side effects attached to individual Neoran Actions round out the picture. With Traits, Jewels, Sigils, and action side effects all in play, the studio's claim that ordering and synergy matter more than the raw action list is easy to take at face value.

## Roster scope and confirmed Neoran

Nara Studio has stated that the base scope promises more than 70 recruitable Neoran, with campaign stretch goals adding more. That is a scope commitment rather than a published roster.

Confirmed creatures include:

- **Pero** — an extremely underdeveloped creature that conveys thought only by spinning around. Peros live on the branch where they are born, fall to the ground at maturity, and sit at the bottom of the valley food chain. Fear appears to be beyond them.
- **Yakid** — a durable coastal clan creature that serves its clan's Wakiza.
- **Sagira** — referenced in the trailer example as the source of the Synapse action Silent Assassin.
- **Biskem** — shown in the trailer as a wild enemy Neoran capable of Cloudbreak.

No stat blocks, signature move lists, or evolution trees have been released for these creatures, so anything beyond the above would be speculation.

## Release window, platforms, and language support

Wonderful Neoran Valley is targeting sometime in 2028. No exact date has been announced. Confirmed platforms are PC via Steam, PlayStation 5, and Nintendo Switch 2. The game will launch in English, French, Spanish, and Italian, with the possibility of additional localizations later.

The Knight is voiced by Troy Baker and Iris by Ben Starr. Nara Studio's Kickstarter campaign reached its funding goal in ten minutes, according to the studio's own milestone post; the campaign page is the place to check current figures rather than repeating numbers that move. Kickstarter perks include the official artbook, and merchandise tied to the campaign will ship closer to release.

## FAQ

**Is Wonderful Neoran Valley a monster collector or a monster taming game?**
It is officially described as a roguelite monster collector. Recruitment happens through dialogue on the battlefield rather than a capture device, so the familiar taming loop — weakening a creature and throwing something at it — does not apply. Neoran are intelligent, many can speak, and they join on their own terms.

**How many Neoran can you recruit in Wonderful Neoran Valley?**
The base scope promises more than 70 recruitable Neoran, with campaign stretch goals adding more. The full roster has not been published, and confirmed creatures so far include Pero, Yakid, Sagira, and Biskem.

**Do Neoran evolve?**
Yes. The official FAQ confirms evolution, but no evolution paths, level requirements, or branch details have been shown.

**Does every Neoran have a unique Synapse action?**
According to Nara Studio, yes — each Neoran has its own Synapse action with a unique effect and animation. Since these actions consume Neura charges that build over turns, a recruit is also a new time-manipulation option for your party.
