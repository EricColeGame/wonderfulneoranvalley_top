## Recruit Through Dialogue, Not Capture

The biggest tip is to rethink the usual monster-collector loop. Neoran are intelligent creatures, and many can speak. Recruitment happens through dialogue on the battlefield, not by throwing a capture device. That makes conversation and battlefield decisions central to building a party.

Guides that assume a throw-a-device capture loop do not match the confirmed design. Treat recruitment as a social and tactical choice, not a matter of weakening a creature and tossing hardware at it.

## Use the Timeline Before Acting

The Knight has foresight, and combat uses the Timeline, which shows enemy actions several turns ahead. Instead of reacting one turn at a time, read the Timeline before committing your plan.

This is the clearest confirmed combat tip so far: plan around what enemies will do several turns out, not just the current turn. If you ignore the Timeline, you are giving up the main information advantage the design provides.

## Manage Synapse and Neura Charges

Synapse lets party members act during the planning phase and consumes Neura charges that build over turns. The practical tip is to track both the planning phase and your charge economy.

Treat Synapse as a timed resource, not a free extra action. Because Neura charges build over turns, spending them early can change what you can do later. The confirmed material does not provide exact charge costs or action lists, so those details remain unconfirmed.

## Build Around Jewels, Sigils, and Traits

Team building has three confirmed layers. Jewels are team-wide passive artifacts forged by Iris. Sigils modify individual Neoran Actions. Traits are passive modifiers, and some have field-wide effects.

A useful tip is to decide whether a build needs a team-wide effect, a specific Action upgrade, or a field-wide trait. Specific Jewel, Sigil, and Trait combinations are not yet confirmed, so avoid treating any tier list as final.
