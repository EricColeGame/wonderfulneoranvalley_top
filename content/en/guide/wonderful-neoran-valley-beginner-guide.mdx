**Wonderful Neoran Valley** is a narrative-driven roguelite monster collector from Nara Studio, and it asks more of you than a typical creature-catching game. You do not throw a device at a weakened animal. You talk to intelligent creatures on the battlefield and recruit them. You do not react to enemy turns as they happen — the Knight sees them coming and you plan around them.

This beginner guide covers only what Nara Studio has actually confirmed: the Timeline, Synapse, Jewels, Sigils, Traits, dialogue-based recruitment, and the 2028 release window. Anything the studio has not detailed is marked as unconfirmed rather than guessed.

## The Timeline is the whole battle

Every fight in Wonderful Neoran Valley revolves around the Timeline. Because the Knight can sense hostile intent moments before it happens, the game shows you what enemy Neoran intend to do several turns in advance. Your job is not to survive turn one — it is to read the schedule and break it.

Enemy Neoran often build combos inside their own portion of the Timeline. Some Jewels and Traits also apply global effects to every creature on the field, enemy included. Studying a pattern tells you what is coming, why, and where the plan can be interrupted.

### Order and synergy beat raw power

The site is explicit that choosing which actions to play is only half the decision. Their order and their synergy, which shift from run to run, are what actually decide outcomes. A strong action in the wrong slot can waste a turn you needed later.

### Spending today costs you tomorrow

Resources spent in one fight are missing from the next. The developers frame this with a blunt example: sacrificing a Sagira to save a Pero is a bad trade if that Pero cannot handle what follows. Treat every run as a chain of fights, not a single duel.

## Synapse: acting before the Timeline starts

Synapse is the signature mechanic born from the Knight and Iris working together. Iris binds the Neura flow in the Knight's arm to the Neoran on the field, which extends the Knight's time-altering gift to the whole party. Your creatures can then act during the planning phase, before the Timeline begins.

Each recruited Neoran brings its own Synapse action with a unique effect and animation, so recruitment is also how you add new ways to bend time. Synapse actions consume Neura charges that build turn after turn. Spend early for tempo or save for a decisive swing — that choice can flip a battle.

## Jewels, Sigils, and Traits

These three systems define your build, and beginners should learn to tell them apart immediately.

**Jewels** are Neura artifacts forged by Iris that passively grant team-wide bonuses. They are the backbone of a solid strategy, and there are many kinds. The Steam Jewel shown in the trailer boosts Blue attacks and Red attacks when placed next to each other in your Timeline — a clear hint that positioning matters as much as selection.

**Sigils** modify individual Neoran Actions, raising their strength or changing how they work. They are less common than Jewels but more adaptable, and they stack into a huge number of effect combinations.

**Traits** are passive modifiers, some with effects that apply to every creature on the field.

## Recruitment is a conversation

Neoran are intelligent creatures, and most of them can speak the human language. Recruitment happens through dialogue on the battlefield. There is no capture device, no weakening ritual, and no capture rate to farm. If you are arriving from other monster collectors, unlearn the throwing arm.

The confirmed roster includes creatures with real personalities rather than stat blocks. Pero has an extremely underdeveloped brain and conveys thoughts only by spinning, sitting at the bottom of the valley food chain while remaining utterly fearless. Yakid is a durable coastal clan creature that serves its clan's Wakiza. Neoran do evolve, per the official FAQ.
